% Narrative adapted from the Part A final presentation.

A Vietnamese student opens her essay with her grandfather's proverb,
``water wears down stone'', and builds her argument the way many Asian
writing traditions teach: context first, conclusion arrived at rather
than announced. A human examiner recognises the skill. An AI grader,
trained overwhelmingly on Western text, may see only a missing thesis
statement and mark her down. The penalty is not for weak English but
for the shape of her reasoning.

As artificial intelligence increasingly grades essays that decide
scholarships, visas, and university places, this hidden cultural
penalty could affect students worldwide. This thesis measures the penalty
and proposes a practical fix called CAMPS: instead of one AI judge, a
panel of AI judges, each reading the essay through a different cultural
lens, whose scores are combined. When the judges strongly disagree, the
essay is flagged for a human teacher; the disagreement itself becomes
a bias detector.
